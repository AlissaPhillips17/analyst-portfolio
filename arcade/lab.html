01 / MEASUREMENT

Event quality & metric contracts

A purchase event is not always a purchase. Retries, duplicate triggers, and missing order matches can make a healthy-looking dashboard tell the wrong story.

In this synthetic ecommerce example, I define what conversion actually counts, reconcile client events against completed orders, and use SQL to expose duplicates and unmatched records. The contract documents required fields, exclusions, and the handling of late events.

The insight

Distinct event IDs do not guarantee distinct orders. Count each completed order once, and keep revenue reporting separate from session conversion when a tracked session is missing.

Inside the project: metric definitions · reconciliation SQL · QA rules · attribution tradeoffs.

02 / EXPERIMENTATION

Experiment decision analysis

A higher conversion rate is a finding, not the whole launch decision. This synthetic checkout experiment asks whether a new experience improves completed-order conversion and what else needs to be true before rollout.

I define visitor-level assignment and the eligible population, calculate absolute lift and an approximate confidence interval in Python, and compare revenue per visitor. The analysis produces a decision memo that separates the observed effect from unresolved business risks.

The insight

A positive conversion interval does not establish that revenue or refunds are safe. Aggregate revenue gives a point estimate; assignment balance, tracking integrity, refund lag, and guardrail uncertainty still need review.

Inside the project: reproducible analysis · synthetic cohorts · uncertainty estimates · rollout considerations.
